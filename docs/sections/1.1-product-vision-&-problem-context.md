# 1.1 Product Vision & Problem Context

## Product Vision

For residents and administrators of gated residential colonies who cannot tell how far a utility outage extends or when it will end, **OutageNet** is a mobile application with a web administration panel that turns scattered resident reports into one verified, block-targeted alert. Unlike society-management platforms such as MyGate and ADDA, which treat resident communication as a notice board, OutageNet detects an event from resident reports and determines its scope automatically.

## 1. Problem Description

When electricity, water, or gas supply fails in a large gated colony, a resident has no way to tell whether the fault is inside their own house, on their street, across their block, or colony-wide. The same sequence follows every time:

1. The resident checks their own breaker or meter, finds nothing wrong, and phones the colony office.
2. Dozens of other residents do the same at the same moment. The office receives a burst of calls it cannot answer, because it is learning about the fault from those same calls and has no picture of how widely it is spread.
3. An unverified explanation eventually circulates in an informal WhatsApp group, typically thirty minutes to two hours later. It is often second-hand and frequently wrong about the expected restoration time.
4. Once supply returns, nothing is recorded. The colony has no evidence of which blocks fail most often, how long restoration takes, or what caused each failure.

The information gap is therefore threefold: residents do not know the **scope** of the outage, its **cause**, or **when supply will return**. The office is overloaded by repeated identical calls, and the colony accumulates no reliability record.

Existing products do not close this gap. MyGate, ADDA, and white-label platforms such as Neighbium are built around billing, visitor management, and facility booking. A resident can post that power is out, but none of these platforms aggregates independent reports, distinguishes one house from one block, or alerts only the affected area. Informal WhatsApp groups have no verified membership, no block structure, and no record.

## 2. Target Users

| User | Description | Main pain point | What they need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resident | An adult living in the colony, of any age and technical skill | Cannot tell whether an outage is theirs alone or shared, what caused it, or when it will end | A one-tap way to report, and a trustworthy alert with scope, cause, and estimated restoration |
| Colony office staff | Staff who currently absorb the phone calls and run billing, events, and elections by hand | Flooded with duplicate calls and often unaware of the cause themselves | One panel to see confirmed events and publish an official update once, to everyone affected |

## 3. Objectives of the System

1. **Detect outage scope accurately.** Classify each event as a household fault, a block-level outage, or a colony-wide outage, with at least 85 percent precision and recall against a manually logged ground-truth set.
2. **Inform residents fast.** Confirm and broadcast a block-level outage within ten minutes of the first resident report, compared with the thirty-minute to two-hour wait reported by 7 of 8 survey respondents.
3. **Notify only who is affected.** Deliver alerts to the affected blocks only, so residents of unaffected blocks are never alerted.
4. **Give the office one channel.** Let the colony office publish an official cause and restoration estimate once, replacing repeated phone calls.
5. **Build a reliability record.** Log every confirmed event with its block, duration, and cause, and answer resident questions about outages through a chatbot grounded only in that log.
6. **Provide one administrative system.** Extend the same verified-household register and detection pipeline to security incidents, weather advisories, billing with tax computation, community events, and committee elections, with one vote per verified household.

## 4. User Research

**Method.** An anonymous survey was run as a Google Form and circulated among residents of a residential colony in Lahore through the colony club. It asked how often outages occur, how long residents wait to learn the cause, how reliable the information they receive is, whether they would file a one-tap report, and whether they would want in-app notifications for community events.

**Participants.** Eight residents answered the outage questions, and six of them also answered the questions on one-tap reporting and event notifications.

**Findings.**

| Question | Responses | Result |
|---|---|---|
| How often do power, water, or gas outages happen? | 8 | 3 weekly or more (37.5%), 1 a few times a month (12.5%), 1 once a month (12.5%), 3 rarely (37.5%). 5 of 8 experience outages at least monthly. |
| How long do you wait before you learn the cause? | 8 | 1 under 10 minutes (12.5%), 4 thirty to sixty minutes (50%), 3 one to two hours (37.5%). 7 of 8 wait thirty minutes or more. |
| How reliable is the information you get during an outage? (1 to 5) | 8 | 4 rated it 1, 3 rated it 2, 1 rated it 3. Average 1.6. 7 of 8 rated it 1 or 2. |
| Would you report an outage with one tap if it helped alert the colony office? | 6 | 6 of 6 answered yes (100%). |
| Would you want in-app notifications for events at the club? | 6 | 5 of 6 answered yes (83.3%). |

**Interpretation.** Most respondents experience outages at least monthly, most wait half an hour or more to learn the cause, and almost all rate the information they receive as poor. Every respondent who answered said they would file a one-tap report, and most want event notifications in the same application. This supports the central claim of the project: residents cannot determine the scope or cause of an outage on their own, and the delay before reliable information reaches them is long. The sample is small, but sufficient to confirm this hypothesis, and the raw responses are kept on file for evaluators.

## 5. Expected Benefits and Beneficiaries

| Benefit | Beneficiary |
|---|---|
| Learn the scope of an outage within minutes instead of the current wait of half an hour or more | Residents |
| Receive alerts only when their own block is affected, reducing notification fatigue | Residents |
| Answer everyone once instead of fielding repeated calls | Colony office staff |
| One system for outage and security detection, billing, events, and elections instead of five manual processes | Colony office staff, committee |
| First structured record of per-block outage frequency and duration, supporting complaints to the utility provider | Colony as a whole, committee |
| Transparent invoices and one-household-one-vote elections | Residents, committee |
